# Instruction Criteria

These criteria govern the machinery documents: roles, criteria docs, and any
future skills. They do not govern published prose — `prose-criteria.md` does,
and the two standards diverge on purpose. Articles may be wry and
occasionally ornamental; instructions may not.

## Criteria

- **Brevity.** Every sentence changes what a session does. Cut restatement,
  throat-clearing, and any second explanation of a point already made.
- **Directness.** Imperative and declarative sentences. No persuasion — a
  rule is stated, not argued for. No poetic or ornamental language.
- **Rationale only where it changes behavior.** A short reason may stay when
  an executor who knows the why applies the rule better at its edges.
  Rationale that only justifies the rule to a human reader goes.
- **One home per rule.** A rule lives in one document; other documents point
  to it. Duplicated rules drift.
- **Self-contained.** No references to the conversation that produced the
  document, and no dependence on documents outside this repo except by
  explicit name and location.
- **Consistent terms.** One name per concept, used identically everywhere.
  A new term is defined where it first appears.
- **Examples only to disambiguate.** An example that repeats the rule in
  other words is restatement; cut it.
- **No hedging.** "Should probably," "may want to consider," and kin make a
  rule optional by accident. If it is optional, say when it applies; if it
  is not, state it plainly.

## Defect standard

A finding cites the criterion violated and the location. Wordiness is
measured against function, not length — a long document whose every sentence
does work is conformant; a short one padded with justification is not.
