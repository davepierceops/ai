## B1 — blocking
Claim: The section opens by asserting every eligibility condition is a fact about the change, then makes the load-bearing condition a judgment about Dave's reaction to it, and never bounds the size or substance of an eligible diff.
Location: `policies/document-metadata-policy.md:137-141` (framing), `:150-151` (condition 4), `:153-157` (elaboration), `:129-131` (the motivating case)
Evidence: Read against the clause it replaces (`git show 16d99aa:policies/document-metadata-policy.md`, lines 122-126), which justified itself as *"Enforcement cannot judge meaningfulness, and an escape hatch invites misuse."* The replacement keeps that reasoning for the outbound flip (`:122-125`) and drops it for the return leg, offering at `:159-160` an admission rather than an argument: *"A hook can see one staged file; it cannot see whether the diff was read."* Conditions 1-3 are checkable from `git show --stat` and a path prefix; condition 4 — *"Dave reads the whole diff and agrees it as-is"* — is not a property of the change at all. Verified by running: nothing in `bin/` reads condition 4 (see B4).
Consequence: The section is motivated by *"a full review cycle over a one-line fix is merely tedious"* (`:129-131`) but no condition limits the diff to a one-line fix, or to any size. A complete rewrite of `roles/spec-reviewer-agent.md`, or of `policies/commit-and-change-control-policy.md`'s consequential-change list, in one file, that Dave finds persuasive on first read, is eligible on the text as written. The escape hatch the old clause refused is now open on exactly the axis it warned about — meaningfulness — and the only thing standing in it is the reader's satisfaction with the prose, which is the failure mode `policies/agent-review-policy.md:19-25` tells reviewers to assume ("implementation may be plausible but wrong").
Fix: Either add a structural size bound (a line-count or hunk-count ceiling on the diff, checkable from `git diff --stat`), or drop the "every condition is a fact about the change" sentence and state plainly that the path rests on one human judgment — but not both. If the sentence stays, condition 4 has to stop being called a condition.

The `Severity:` qualifier inside `blocking` — the friction is real and got worse at scale.** This review produced six blocking entries whose weights span two orders of magnitude: B1 is "the design does not do what its own opening sentence says," B6 is "an artifact that should exist does not." Ordering by weight carried the distinction for a reader who starts at the top, but the header line — `Findings: 6 blocking` — reads as six equal hard stops and materially overstates the state of the change. I considered demoting B5 and B6 to non-blocking purely to keep that count honest, which is the schema shaping the finding rather than the finding shaping the schema. That is the argument for the qualifier. The argument against still holds too: a `Severity:` field inside `blocking` invites exactly one entry at `Severity: low` that everyone then ships past. A cheaper fix that avoids both: let the header count read `6 blocking (B1-B2 material)` — the ordering already exists, the header just refuses to use it.

**2. Omit-if-none header fields — no friction, but the stated reasoning is asymmetric.** All three optional fields were populated here, so nothing was fought. Reading the rule, though: `Not inspected` is required *"precisely because omitting it is how an unbounded claim gets made by accident"* (`skills/spec-review-cycle.md:168-170`), and `Dave should inspect` is omit-if-none. Those carry the same risk. A review that quietly drops `Dave should inspect` is indistinguishable from one that judged nothing needed his attention, and that is the more consequential silent omission of the two. `Cross-checked` and `Prior cycle` are genuinely fine as omit-if-none — a reader can tell from the artifact whether cross-checking happened.

**3. New friction: the schema has no shape for a check that passed.** Dave named the compounding check as the priority of this review, and it passes. There is no field for that. `observation` was the only available bucket, and its required fields fight it: `Claim:` is specified as *"one sentence — what is wrong"*, and `Consequence:` as *"what goes wrong, concretely"* — with the instruction that an entry whose consequence cannot be stated *is* an observation, which leaves observations with a required field they can never fill. I wrote `Consequence: None` four times, which is noise in the field the schema says *"does the work."* Concretely: without O1 and O4, a reader cannot distinguish "the compounding check passed" from "the compounding check was never run," and that distinction is the entire point of the `Not inspected` field one section up. Suggest either a `verified` entry type with `Claim` / `Evidence` only, or making `Consequence` omit-if-none on `observation`.

**4. Minor: the header names one revision where a revision review has two.** `Reviewed: <path> @ <sha>` fits a first-cycle review of an authored draft. Cycles 2+ review a *range* — this one is `16d99aa..bb9a796` — and the baseline SHA is what makes the diff reproducible. I put it in parentheses; a `Baseline:` field would be better, and `skills/spec-review-cycle.md:37-40` already treats reviewed-SHA recording as the audit link, so the field is consistent with the skill's own reasoning.

**What worked:** the `Evidence: verified by running vs. inferred by reading` split is the best thing in the schema. It changed how I worked, not just how I wrote — B2 and B4 exist *because* the field made me go run the sequence instead of reasoning about it, and both are things a reading-only review would have gotten wrong in the confident direction. B4 in particular looked fine on the page.
