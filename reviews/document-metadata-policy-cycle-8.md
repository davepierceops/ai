## B1 — blocking
Claim: The Eligibility framing sentence still declares condition 5 the only human judgment, which contradicts the criterion-primary condition 3 that W-1 was written to install.
Location: `policies/document-metadata-policy.md:140-141`, against `:157-172`
Evidence: **Verified by running.** The framing sentence is untouched by both revisions (`git diff 26998e5 086ff4b -- policies/document-metadata-policy.md` produces no hunk covering `:140`): *"Conditions 1–4 are facts about the change. Condition 5 is a human judgment, and it is the only one:"*. Condition 3 at `:163-172` now reads *"**The criterion decides, and the list below is a floor rather than a boundary.**… **When it is unclear whether a document states a gate, a hard stop, or an enforcement rule, it is ineligible**, mirroring the commit policy's 'when in doubt, consequential.'"* A rule that has to name what happens when its application is unclear is a judgment by construction. At the baseline the two agreed: `ef4438b` still said *"The class is **enumerated, not judged** — a document not named here is not excluded by this condition"*, which is a fact about the change, checkable by path equality. W-1 inverted the sentence it was consistent with and left the framing standing.
Consequence: A reader who takes the framing at its word applies condition 3 mechanically — check the list, and if the document is not on it, proceed — which is precisely the enumeration-only semantics E2 rejected ("the criterion is primary and the named list is a non-exhaustive floor"). The framing is the sentence a reader uses to decide *how* to apply the conditions, so it does not merely sit beside the fix; it licenses the behaviour the fix exists to prevent, and it is upstream of the conditions it frames. Concretely: `context-sets/spec-and-change-discipline.md` states the Spec Reviewer hard gate and is not on the floor list. Under the criterion it is ineligible. Under the framing sentence it is a fact-checkable miss and eligible. Two rules in the same section return opposite answers for the same document.
Fix: Restate the framing to match what the conditions now are — e.g. *"Conditions 1, 2 and 4 are facts about the change. Conditions 3 and 5 require judgment"* — or delete it. The sentence has been the defect in three consecutive gates (cycle-5 B1: wrong about condition 4; cycle-7 N1: wrong about conditions 2 and 3; now wrong about condition 3 again). Deleting it removes a second copy of a list that is already stated beneath it, which is the durable fix; restating it buys a fourth round of the same drift.

## N1 — non-blocking
Claim: The paragraph closing the Eligibility section still describes conditions 1–4 as a structural bound, which condition 3 no longer is.
Location: `policies/document-metadata-policy.md:222-225`
Evidence: **Verified by running**; the passage is untouched by both revisions. It reads: *"Enforcement checks none of this and cannot. A hook can count staged files and changed lines; it cannot see whether the diff was read. Conditions 1–4 bound how much an unread diff could do; condition 5 is what makes the path a review at all."* Under the baseline text a hook genuinely could decide condition 3 — it was path equality against a closed list, and `bin/aimeta/scope.py` already demonstrates the pattern of reading a list out of this policy at runtime. Under `8ba2e84` it cannot: `:174` introduces the list as *"The class includes, at minimum:"*, so a path's absence from it decides nothing.
Consequence: Smaller than B1 because it overstates rather than contradicts, but it is the paragraph that states the residual risk, and it now claims a bound stronger than the one that exists. Conditions 1, 2 and 4 bound an unread diff; condition 3 bounds it only insofar as whoever applies it exercises the judgment correctly — which makes the path rest on two human judgments, not one. That is a fair design, and the document should say so where it accounts for its own limits.
Fix: *"Conditions 1, 2 and 4 bound how much an unread diff could do; conditions 3 and 5 are judgments, and they are what make the path a review at all."* One sentence, and it resolves with B1's fix as a pair.
