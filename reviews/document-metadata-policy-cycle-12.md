## N1 — non-blocking
Claim: The doc-only route imports two of the three clauses of the expedited path's condition 1 and is silent on the third — whether the content revision must be a **single commit**.
Location: `policies/document-metadata-policy.md:334-338`, against `:140-143`; `decisions/log.md:61-69`
Evidence: Verified by reading. Expedited condition 1 carries three bounds: a single commit, exactly one in-scope document, and no other tracked path — "a revision spread across two commits" escalates explicitly (`:143`). The B1 rewrite names the second ("covers exactly one in-scope document, as the expedited path does") and the third ("per the expedited path's 'no other tracked path' rule") and does not mention the first. `DEC-000040` is silent on it too: it withdraws the single-file override and retains the size override, naming neither the single-commit clause nor `DEC-000030`, which was equally silent. The doc-only conditions list (`:292-317`) contains no commit-count condition. The only signal is the Sequence's singular phrasing — "content commit, then the log entry naming that SHA" (`:329-330`).
Consequence: Where the route's whole point is a document "of any size, new or revised", a multi-commit content revision is the expected case, not the exotic one — a long co-authored document drafted over a session naturally lands in several commits. An executor now has to decide whether the unmentioned clause was dropped by intent or by oversight, and the answer changes the record: on the single-commit reading the drafting has to be squashed before the log entry; on the other reading the cited SHA names only the last slice of the revision, so an auditor running `git show <cited sha>` sees part of what was agreed rather than all of it. Naming two of three clauses makes the omission read as deliberate, which is the more expensive of the two wrong answers to guess.
Fix: One clause in `:334-338` settling it — either "in a single commit, as the expedited path requires", or, if a multi-commit revision is intended to be admissible, that the cited SHA is the last content commit and the agreed diff is the range from the document's prior agreed SHA. If the answer is the second, it is a widening of what `DEC-000040` decided and wants a line in that entry's successor rather than only in the policy.
Related: N3
